# ManaposeGP — Australian Legal & Regulatory Compliance Analysis

## Executive Summary

This document analyzes ManaposeGP's compliance position under Australian healthcare law. The platform operates as a **health information and decision-support tool** — it does NOT diagnose, prescribe, or replace clinical judgment. However, several features edge into regulated territory requiring immediate attention.

**Overall risk rating: MODERATE — actionable with clear mitigation steps.**

## 2. Feature-by-Feature Legal Analysis

### 2.1 AI Chatbot (Patient Mode) — 🟡 MODERATE RISK

**Current Implementation:**
- Patient asks health questions → GPT-4o responds with educational content
- System prompt: "You never diagnose, prescribe, or replace medical advice"
- Cites Jean Hailes, AMS, RACGP sources
- Safety guardrails: urgent symptoms → Lifeline/000

**Legal Issues:**
1. **TGA Software as Medical Device (SaMD)**: If the chatbot provides patient-specific advice that could influence clinical decisions, it may be classified as a medical device requiring TGA registration.
2. **Duty of Care**: Providing health information creates a duty of care to ensure accuracy.
3. **Misleading/Deceptive Conduct** (Australian Consumer Law s18): Incorrect AI responses could constitute misleading health claims.

**Red Flag Assessment:**
- The chatbot DOES have safety disclaimers ✅
- The chatbot DOES cite sources ✅
- The chatbot DOES have crisis redirects ✅
- The chatbot DOES NOT perform individual risk assessment or recommend specific treatments ✅

**Verdict: Currently LOW risk — but requires monitoring as AI capabilities expand.**

**Mitigation:**
- [x] Every response includes: "I am an AI health education tool, not a doctor. Discuss treatment decisions with your GP."
- [x] Crisis detection: suicidal ideation, chest pain, postmenopausal bleeding → immediate Lifeline/000 redirect
- [x] **ADDED**: Explicit consent checkbox on first chat use (see `src/components/health/chat-consent-dialog.tsx`)
- [ ] **PENDING**: Response audit log — store all AI responses for 7 years (clinical record retention)
- [ ] **PENDING**: Confidence scoring — clearly label responses as "Evidence-based (AMS/RACGP)" vs "General health information"

### 2.2 AI Chatbot (GP Clinical Mode) — 🔴 HIGHER RISK

**Current Implementation:**
- GP queries clinical guidelines → GPT-4o cites AMS/RACGP/eTG
- Drug interaction checker uses GPT-4o
- Risk calculators (FRAX, cardiovascular) use simplified algorithms
- SOAP note generator creates clinical documentation
- Referral letter generator creates medical correspondence

**Legal Issues:**
1. **Medical Indemnity**: GPs using AI-assisted clinical tools MUST verify their indemnity insurance covers AI-assisted decision-making. MDA National and Avant have specific requirements.
2. **Clinical Documentation Standards**: SOAP notes generated by AI must meet the RACGP Standards for health records. AI-generated content must be explicitly reviewed and signed off.
3. **Drug Interaction Liability**: If the AI misses a drug interaction that causes patient harm, who is liable?
4. **Risk Calculator Accuracy**: Simplified FRAX/CVD calculators carry a disclaimer but GPs may rely on them.

**Red Flag Assessment:**
- GP chat DOES cite sources ✅
- Risk calculators DO have disclaimers ✅
- Drug interaction checker uses GPT-4o (not a validated database) ⚠️ **RED FLAG**
- SOAP notes are marked "AI-ASSISTED — REQUIRES GP REVIEW" ✅
- No audit trail of GP verification of AI output ⚠️ **RED FLAG**

**Verdict: MODERATE-HIGH risk — drug interaction checker and audit trail need immediate attention.**

**Mitigation:**
- [x] All clinical outputs marked "AI-ASSISTED — REQUIRES GP REVIEW"
- [x] Source citations on guidelines
- [x] **ADDED**: GP verification audit trail — AI-generated consultations now have `ai_verified_at` and `ai_verified_by` columns with API endpoint at `/api/gp/consultations/verify` for timestamped GP sign-off
- [x] **ADDED**: Indemnity insurance fields on GP registration (provider, policy number, expiry date)
- [ ] **CRITICAL**: Replace GPT-4o drug interaction checker with a validated database (e.g., AMH Interaction Checker, Stockley's, or licensed API from NPS MedicineWise)
- [ ] **ADD**: Prominent disclaimer on every GP-facing page: "AI tools are decision SUPPORT only. Final clinical decisions and documentation sign-off remain the GP's professional responsibility."

### 2.3 Patient-GP Data Sharing — 🟡 MODERATE RISK

**Current Implementation:**
- Patient grants consent via `patient_consents` table
- GP accesses patient health data after consent
- Symptom journal, health metrics, medications, screening results shared

**Legal Issues:**
1. **Privacy Act — APP 6 (Use and Disclosure)**: Health data sharing must have explicit, informed consent.
2. **APP 11 (Security)**: Health data must be protected against unauthorized access.
3. **Notifiable Data Breaches**: If health data is breached, OAIC notification is mandatory within 30 days.

**Red Flag Assessment:**
- Consent system exists ✅
- Data encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) ✅
- Data encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3) ✅
- Consent types: full_access, summary_only, symptom_data, metrics_data ✅
- **No patient consent revocation audit trail** ⚠️
- **No data breach notification mechanism** ⚠️

**Verdict: MODERATE risk — consent system is well-designed but missing audit trail.**

**Mitigation:**
- [x] Granular consent types
- [x] Encrypted data storage
- [x] **ADDED**: Consent audit logging — `consent_audit_logs` table tracks every grant, accept, revoke with actor type, actor ID, and timestamps
- [ ] **PENDING**: Data breach response plan document
- [ ] **PENDING**: Privacy policy must explicitly describe health data sharing with GPs
- [ ] **PENDING**: Patient must be able to see exactly what data their GP can access

### 2.4 Health Data Storage (Apple HealthKit) — 🟡 MODERATE RISK

**Current Implementation:**
- Apple Health/Watch data syncs to ManaposeGP
- HealthKit types: heart rate, sleep, temperature, cycles, etc.
- Data stored in `health_metrics` table in Neon Postgres (Sydney region)

**Legal Issues:**
1. **Apple HealthKit Guidelines**: Prohibit using HealthKit data for advertising or selling to data brokers. ✅ App complies.
2. **Privacy Act — APP 3 (Collection)**: Only collect health data that is reasonably necessary. ⚠️ Collecting ALL HealthKit data types may exceed "reasonably necessary."
3. **Cross-border Data**: Neon Postgres is US-based. Is the Sydney region Australian soil? Verify data sovereignty.

**Red Flag Assessment:**
- HealthKit permission granular ✅
- Data stored encrypted ✅
- **Neon data sovereignty unclear** ⚠️

**Verdict: MODERATE — verify data sovereignty and collection necessity.**

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] **VERIFY**: Neon Sydney region — is data stored on Australian soil? If not, move to an Australian-hosted PostgreSQL provider or Vercel Postgres (Sydney).
- [ ] **ADD**: Data retention policy — specify how long health data is kept (recommend: 7 years from last consultation per clinical record guidelines, or patient-requested deletion)

### 2.5 Telehealth / Teleconferencing — 🟢 LOW RISK (currently)

**Current Implementation:**
- No built-in video/audio teleconferencing
- Chat-based consultation via AI
- Appointment scheduling

**Legal Issues:**
1. **MBS Telehealth Items**: If GPs use the platform for telehealth consultations, MBS item numbers (91890, 91891 for video; 92745, 92746 for phone) must be used correctly.
2. **Medical Board Guidelines**: Telehealth consultations must meet the same standard of care as in-person. GPs must verify patient identity and ensure appropriate follow-up.

**Verdict: LOW risk currently — no built-in teleconferencing. This changes if video consultations are added.**

**Mitigation (future-proofing):**
- [ ] **PLAN**: If adding video consultations: implement patient identity verification, end-to-end encryption, waiting room functionality, and MBS item number guidance
- [ ] **PLAN**: Ensure the video platform meets RACGP security standards (not consumer-grade like unsecured Zoom)

### 2.6 GP Registration & AHPRA Verification — 🔴 HIGH RISK

**Current Implementation:**
- GP registers via `/gp-onboard` with name, practice, AHPRA number, email
- Admin verifies manually
- No automated AHPRA verification

**Legal Issues:**
1. **AHPRA Registration Check**: The platform allows anyone to register as a GP. The AHPRA number field is optional. There is no automated check against the AHPRA Register of Practitioners.
2. **Title Protection** (Health Practitioner Regulation National Law s113-119): Only AHPRA-registered medical practitioners can use the title "GP" or "General Practitioner" in connection with health services.
3. **Liability**: If an unregistered person gains GP access and provides clinical care, the platform could be liable.

**Red Flag Assessment:**
- AHPRA number is optional ⚠️ **RED FLAG**
- No automated verification ⚠️ **RED FLAG**
- Manual admin verification exists ✅ but insufficient
- No periodic re-verification ⚠️

**Verdict: HIGH risk — AHPRA verification must be mandatory and automated.**

**Mitigation:**
- [x] **DONE** (Jul 2026): AHPRA number mandatory for GP registration
- [x] **DONE** (Jul 2026): Indemnity insurance fields added (provider, policy number, expiry date)
- [ ] **CRITICAL**: Integrate AHPRA public register API for automated verification: `https://www.ahpra.gov.au/Registration/Registers-of-Practitioners.aspx`
- [ ] **ADD**: Require GP to upload proof of identity and medical registration
- [ ] **ADD**: Periodic re-verification (every 12 months) — check AHPRA status hasn't changed

### 2.7 Appointment Booking & Notifications — 🟡 MODERATE RISK

**Current Implementation:**
- GP can schedule appointments for patients
- Invite links to join platform
- No automated notifications yet

**Legal Issues:**
1. **Spam Act 2003**: Commercial electronic messages require consent. Appointment reminders are generally "designated commercial electronic messages" — consent is implied by the patient relationship.
2. **Privacy**: Appointment data is health information subject to APPs.

**Verdict: LOW-MODERATE — currently low risk, increases if automated notifications are added.**

**Mitigation:**
- [ ] **ADD**: Unsubscribe mechanism on all notification emails
- [ ] **ADD**: Explicit consent checkbox for SMS/email reminders
- [ ] **ADD**: Message content must not reveal clinical information in subject lines or SMS previews

## 4. Telehealth / Teleconferencing — Legal Standpoint

### 4.1 Current Australian Telehealth Framework (2026)

| Aspect | Requirement |
|--------|------------|
| **Eligibility** | GP must have an existing clinical relationship with the patient (generally — some exceptions for COVID-era provisions) |
| **MBS Items** | Video: 91890 (Level B), 91891 (Level C), 91892 (Level D). Phone: 92745 (Level B), 92746 (Level C) |
| **Patient Consent** | Verbal consent at start of consultation, documented in clinical notes |
| **Identity Verification** | GP must confirm patient identity — visual (video) or question-based (phone) |
| **Clinical Standard** | Same standard of care as in-person consultation |
| **Privacy** | Consultation must occur in a private setting — not in public |
| **Prescribing** | Schedule 8 and Schedule 4D medicines require additional verification |
| **Follow-up** | GPs must arrange appropriate follow-up care |

### 4.2 ManaposeGP's Position

The platform does NOT currently provide video/audio teleconferencing. If teleconferencing is added:

- The platform becomes a "telehealth platform" subject to RACGP Standards for general practices
- Video must be end-to-end encrypted
- The patient must be able to verify the GP's identity within the platform
- Clinical notes from telehealth consultations must meet the same documentation standards

**Recommendation**: Do NOT add teleconferencing without a full legal review by a health law specialist.

## 8. Conclusion

ManaposeGP is well-positioned as a health information and decision-support platform. The core architecture (disclaimers, source citation, tier-based access, consent management, encryption) is solid. However, **five critical items** require immediate attention before the platform can be considered legally robust:

1. Mandatory AHPRA verification
2. Validated drug interaction checking
3. GP audit trail
4. AI response logging
5. Data sovereignty verification

With these fixes, the platform transitions from **MODERATE-HIGH risk** to **LOW-MODERATE risk** and is well-positioned for clinical use.
